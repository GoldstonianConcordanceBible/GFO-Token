1. Purpose

This file defines the authoritative research-question structure for the Global Social Experiment and its related Web4, Web4 NIL, artificial intelligence governance, education, theology, cultural, healthcare, repository, consulting, and implementation programs.

The register serves five functions:

1. preserve the primary research question;
2. organize the full research agenda;
3. identify the smaller question sets used for specific publications;
4. prevent unsupported scope expansion;
5. connect research questions to evidence, findings, policies, code, and derivative outputs.

The full research agenda contains 226 numbered research questions, one primary research question, six article questions, eight book questions, and eight conference questions.

The full question bank is not intended to be answered in one study or publication.

It functions as the long-term research agenda and canonical repository question bank.

⸻

2. Primary Research Question

PRQ1

How can a version-controlled, AI-assisted governance repository be used to design, test, document, and improve human-centered rules for identity, autonomous agents, tokenized communities, creator compensation, and institutional coordination in an emerging Web4 environment?

All major research activities should identify how they contribute to PRQ1.

A study may investigate only one component of PRQ1, but it must clearly identify that component.

29. Question Admission Procedure

New research questions require:

1. a unique proposed identifier;
2. a clearly written neutral question;
3. a domain;
4. a relationship to PRQ1;
5. a rationale;
6. an identifiable method;
7. evidence needs;
8. risks;
9. dependencies;
10. and repository approval.

New questions must not reuse retired identifiers.

Once assigned, an identifier remains historically traceable.

⸻

30. Question Revision Procedure

A research question may be revised when:

* terminology changes;
* scope is clarified;
* a question contains hidden assumptions;
* the question becomes too broad;
* legal or technical conditions change;
* or new evidence requires subdivision.

Major revisions must preserve:

* the original wording;
* the revised wording;
* the reason for change;
* the approving reviewer;
* the effective version;
* and links to affected findings.

⸻

31. Question Retirement Procedure

A question may be retired when it:

* is fully superseded;
* duplicates another question;
* is no longer relevant;
* is impossible to investigate ethically;
* contains an invalid assumption;
* or falls outside repository scope.

Retirement does not delete the question.

The record should remain in the archive with its reason and replacement identifier.

No derivative output should claim that all 226 questions have been answered.

* which questions it addresses;

35. Canonical Research Question Principle

The research-question register is designed to keep the project answerable, falsifiable, and governable.

The repository must remain capable of distinguishing:

* a question from a conclusion;
* a hypothesis from a finding;
* a model response from evidence;
* a proposed standard from law;
* an implementation plan from an operational control;
* and an unresolved problem from a solved one.
