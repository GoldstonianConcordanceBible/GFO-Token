Overview

This folder contains the canonical research, governance, methodology, and structured knowledge materials for the Global Social Experiment, Web4, Web4 NIL, and related artificial intelligence governance initiatives within the broader GFO ecosystem.

The folder is designed to support:

* a version-controlled research program;
* the development of Web4 as an emerging theoretical construct;
* the Web4 NIL governance framework;
* a modified multi-model LLM Delphi study;
* human-centered identity and autonomous-agent governance;
* DAO authority and reserved-rights policies;
* policy-to-code traceability;
* creator-fee and compensation design;
* legal-claims verification;
* privacy and data governance;
* education, culture, theology, and healthcare research;
* licensed implementation materials;
* and a future prompt-based website backed by approved repository content.

This folder is not the GFO token contract folder.

It is the research and governance layer that documents how identity, autonomous agents, tokenized communities, compensation systems, and institutional rules should be evaluated within the GFO ecosystem.

⸻

Why This Folder Is Inside GFO-token

The Global Social Experiment includes tokenized communities, decentralized governance, machine payments, creator compensation, autonomous agents, and identity-linked digital assets.

Because the GFO ecosystem may implement or reference these systems, the research and governance framework belongs within the broader GFO-token repository as a clearly separated domain.

Its inclusion does not mean that every research proposal is active in the GFO token system.

The repository must distinguish among:

* research questions;
* hypotheses;
* Delphi findings;
* proposed governance standards;
* approved GFO policies;
* implemented technical controls;
* and live deployed systems.

No research statement becomes an operational GFO rule unless it passes the applicable governance, legal, technical, security, and release processes.

Evidence Levels

The recommended evidence scale is:

E0 — Unverified concept
E1 — Researcher or participant interpretation
E2 — Single-source support
E3 — Multiple-source support
E4 — Cross-model Delphi convergence
E5 — Qualified human professional review
E6 — Empirical or technical validation
E7 — Independent replication

LLM consensus should not automatically exceed E4.

Legal, regulatory, privacy, financial, medical, or security claims may require qualified professional review before publication or activation.

LLM Delphi Study

This folder documents a modified Delphi study involving multiple frontier language models.

The study is designed to test whether independently controlled models can converge on governance principles involving:

* human identity;
* AI personas;
* synthetic media;
* autonomous agents;
* tokenized communities;
* creator compensation;
* DAO authority;
* privacy;
* legal verification;
* and policy-to-code conformance.

The three-round process includes:

Round One

Independent model review without exposure to other panel responses.

Round Two

Review of an anonymized synthesis, including opportunities to revise, defend, narrow, or reject initial conclusions.

Round Three

Stability review identifying final consensus, conditional agreement, unresolved questions, dissent, errors, and professional-review requirements.

LLM convergence is treated as structured model agreement, not human expert consensus or professional certification.

Unknown Unknowns

This research program was developed partly to study unknown unknowns.

An unknown unknown is a risk, interaction, capability, or consequence that has not yet been recognized clearly enough to become a formal research question.

The framework distinguishes:

* known knowns: matters currently understood;
* known unknowns: recognized unanswered questions;
* unknown knowns: hidden assumptions or embedded practices;
* unknown unknowns: risks or interactions that have not yet become visible.

The repository cannot eliminate unknown unknowns.

It is intended to make them easier to detect, record, evaluate, and convert into governed research questions.

Web4

Web4 is treated as an emerging and testable research construct.

The current working hypothesis describes environments where some combination of the following interact:

* humans;
* artificial intelligence agents;
* decentralized or portable identity;
* synthetic personas;
* programmable authorization;
* machine-readable consent;
* tokenized coordination;
* machine payments;
* version-controlled governance;
* policy-to-code traceability;
* and human revocation or override rights.

The repository preserves open questions about whether Web4 requires:

* blockchain;
* tokens;
* smart contracts;
* autonomous agents;
* decentralized identity;
* or machine payments.

Web4 may be revised, divided into subconstructs, merged with another framework, or rejected as evidence develops.

Web4 NIL

Web4 NIL is a proposed framework for governing the authorized use of human-linked identity elements in synthetic, agentic, tokenized, and digitally mediated environments.

Potential identity elements include:

* name;
* image;
* likeness;
* voice;
* biography;
* signature;
* writing style;
* avatar;
* persona;
* synthetic media;
* digital replica;
* digital twin;
* memory representation;
* credentials;
* and autonomous agents acting under human-associated authority.

Authorization for one identity element does not automatically authorize another.

Consent should be capable of defining:

* permitted identity elements;
* purpose;
* platform;
* jurisdiction;
* duration;
* compensation;
* modification rights;
* sublicensing rights;
* model-training rights;
* disclosure requirements;
* suspension rights;
* revocation rights;
* termination rights;
* and surviving archival uses.

Documentation Readiness and Activation Readiness

This folder distinguishes between two forms of readiness.

Documentation Readiness

The relevant policies, schemas, agreements, procedures, roles, and records have been drafted and reviewed.

Activation Readiness

The system has completed the legal, technical, security, privacy, operational, and professional-review requirements necessary for live implementation.

A project may be documentation-ready without being activation-ready.

No readiness designation should imply legal compliance, regulatory approval, technical security, financial safety, or professional certification unless independently established.
