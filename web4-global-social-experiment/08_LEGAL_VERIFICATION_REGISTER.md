Purpose

The Legal Verification Register documents every legal, regulatory, judicial, policy, and governmental claim referenced throughout the Global Social Experiment.

Its purpose is to answer:

“Has this legal statement been independently verified?”

The modified multi-model Delphi study demonstrated that frontier language models can incorrectly present proposed legislation, draft guidance, or commentary as binding law.

Accordingly, every legal assertion should pass through a structured verification process before being relied upon for:

* repository policy;
* implementation guidance;
* publications;
* presentations;
* consulting;
* educational materials;
* or public AI retrieval.

⸻

Core Principle

Legal verification is separate from:

* consensus;
* evidence strength;
* popularity;
* model agreement;
* researcher preference;
* and implementation desirability.

A statement may enjoy broad agreement while still being legally inaccurate.

Proposed Law vs Enacted Law

Every legal statement must clearly distinguish:

Enacted Law
↓
Currently Effective

from

Bill
↓
Not Yet Law

and

Agency Guidance
↓
Nonbinding

Failure to distinguish these categories was one of the most important methodological observations emerging from the Delphi study.

Legal Quality Checklist

Before publishing a legal statement, verify:

* jurisdiction identified;
* primary authority located;
* enactment status confirmed;
* current effective date confirmed where applicable;
* secondary sources separated;
* repository interpretation labeled;
* next review scheduled;
* repository version recorded.

Canonical Principle

Legal statements require their own verification pathway.

No amount of model agreement can convert:

* a proposal into enacted law;
* commentary into statute;
* guidance into regulation;
* or repository preference into legal authority.

The retrieval chain remains:

Question
↓
Legal Verification
↓
Primary Authority
↓
Repository Interpretation
↓
Claim
↓
Answer

This register protects the integrity of the repository, supports responsible publication, and reduces the risk of presenting outdated or inaccurate legal information through AI-assisted retrieval.
