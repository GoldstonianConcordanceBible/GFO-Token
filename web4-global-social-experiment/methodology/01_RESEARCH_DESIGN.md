Purpose

This document defines the formal research design for the Global Social Experiment.

The study is structured as a mixed-methods, version-controlled, repository-centered research program that integrates qualitative analysis, documentary evidence, AI-assisted review, and iterative governance refinement.

Unlike traditional studies that conclude after publication, this design assumes that governance knowledge evolves through continuous observation, correction, replication, and public transparency.

⸻

Primary Research Question

How can a version-controlled, AI-assisted governance repository be used to design, test, document, and improve human-centered rules for identity, autonomous agents, tokenized communities, creator compensation, and institutional coordination in an emerging Web4 environment?

This question serves as the organizing principle for every component of the repository.

Research Paradigm

The project intentionally combines multiple research traditions.

Mixed Methods

Integrates qualitative, documentary, comparative, and computational evidence.

⸻

Design Science

Produces governance artifacts intended for evaluation and refinement.

⸻

Grounded Theory

Allows theoretical concepts to emerge through iterative analysis rather than beginning with a fully predetermined framework.

⸻

Repository-Centered Research

Treats version-controlled repositories as both:

* research infrastructure;
* and research data.

⸻

Modified Delphi

Uses multiple independent frontier language models as structured reviewers within a controlled iterative process.

⸻

Implementation Science

Separates theoretical validity from operational readiness.

Research Assumptions

The study assumes:

* governance evolves;
* documentation matters;
* AI review can improve structured critique;
* disagreement has research value;
* provenance increases transparency;
* repository history supports reproducibility;
* and implementation requires more than documentation.

These assumptions remain open to revision.

Success Criteria

The research is considered successful if it demonstrates:

* transparent methodology;
* traceable evidence;
* preserved dissent;
* legal verification;
* reproducibility;
* repository governance;
* and a clearly defined scholarly contribution.

Success is not measured by universal agreement or adoption.

Canonical Principle

The research design establishes how the Global Social Experiment investigates governance—not what conclusions it must reach.

The methodology intentionally allows evidence, structured review, and replication to challenge, refine, or overturn initial assumptions.

The governing sequence is:

Research Question
↓
Evidence
↓
Analysis
↓
Review
↓
Consensus and Dissent
↓
Verification
↓
Repository Release
↓
Publication
↓
Replication

The design therefore treats the repository itself as a continuously evolving research instrument rather than a static archive, ensuring that future discoveries can be incorporated without sacrificing transparency or scholarly rigor.
