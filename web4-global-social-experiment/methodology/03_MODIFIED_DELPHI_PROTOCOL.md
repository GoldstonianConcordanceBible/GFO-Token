Purpose

This document defines the modified Delphi protocol used throughout the Global Social Experiment.

Unlike a traditional Delphi study, which relies on panels of human experts, this protocol uses multiple frontier large language models as structured reviewers operating under controlled conditions.

The objective is not to replace human expertise. Instead, it is to determine whether independent AI systems can contribute useful structured critique, identify overlooked governance issues, expose disagreements, and improve documentation quality when their outputs are evaluated transparently.

⸻

Why Modify the Delphi Method?

The classical Delphi method was designed to:

* collect independent expert opinions;
* reduce groupthink;
* refine judgments through multiple rounds;
* and identify stable areas of agreement.

The Global Social Experiment extends this logic to AI-assisted governance research while recognizing that language models are not equivalent to human subject-matter experts.

The protocol therefore treats LLMs as analytical reviewers, not authoritative decision-makers.

Reviewer Independence

The first principle of the protocol is independent review.

Each model evaluates the governance artifact before seeing the conclusions of other reviewers.

Repository Draft
↓
Reviewer A
Reviewer B
Reviewer C
Reviewer D
↓
Independent Responses

This stage minimizes anchoring and encourages diverse reasoning.

⸻

Round One

Objective

Generate independent assessments.

Each reviewer receives substantially the same material and evaluates:

* strengths;
* weaknesses;
* missing controls;
* methodological concerns;
* governance recommendations;
* implementation risks;
* legal observations;
* and future work.

Outputs are preserved without modification.

No reviewer sees another review during this phase.

⸻

Round Two

Objective

Controlled reconsideration.

Each reviewer receives an anonymized synthesis of Round One.

The reviewer is asked to:

* reconsider earlier conclusions;
* explain agreement;
* explain disagreement;
* identify overlooked issues;
* and revise recommendations where appropriate.

Round Two measures whether structured reflection changes conclusions without forcing consensus.

⸻

Round Three

Objective

Final stability assessment.

Reviewers examine:

* consolidated findings;
* unresolved disagreements;
* methodological observations;
* legal corrections;
* and proposed repository revisions.

The objective is not unanimous agreement.

The objective is to determine which findings remain stable after repeated review.

⸻

Controlled Replication

After the three primary rounds, a separately controlled reviewer may perform an independent replication.

The replication reviewer:

* does not participate in earlier synthesis;
* evaluates the final architecture independently;
* compares conclusions;
* identifies convergence;
* and documents remaining disagreement.

This additional review provides a check on orchestration effects.

Dissent Preservation

Unlike many review processes, dissent is intentionally retained.

Every unresolved disagreement is documented.

Examples include:

* competing governance models;
* alternative implementation strategies;
* methodological differences;
* legal uncertainty;
* and theoretical disagreement.

Dissent becomes an input to future research rather than something to eliminate.

Legal Verification

Legal observations made during Delphi review require independent verification.

Reviewers may identify:

* statutes;
* regulations;
* proposed legislation;
* agency guidance;
* or commentary.

However, repository adoption requires verification through the Legal Verification Register.

The protocol intentionally separates:

Reviewer Agreement
↓
Legal Accuracy

These are not equivalent.

⸻

Unknown Unknown Discovery

One objective of the protocol is to identify issues that researchers did not anticipate before review.

Examples include:

* hidden governance dependencies;
* undocumented implementation assumptions;
* overlooked consent fields;
* policy conflicts;
* architecture weaknesses;
* and methodological blind spots.

These observations are recorded regardless of whether consensus emerges.

⸻

Data Preservation

The repository preserves:

* original prompts;
* reviewer identities;
* response timestamps;
* synthesized summaries;
* consensus classifications;
* dissent records;
* legal corrections;
* and repository revisions.

Nothing of methodological significance should be overwritten.

⸻

Limitations

The protocol recognizes several limitations.

Language models:

* may hallucinate;
* share overlapping training data;
* may reflect prompt wording;
* may display excessive confidence;
* cannot independently verify external legal authority;
* and do not possess professional accountability.

Accordingly, the protocol treats model outputs as structured analytical input rather than definitive expert judgment.

Success Criteria

The modified Delphi protocol is considered successful if it demonstrates:

* independent review;
* transparent methodology;
* preserved disagreement;
* documented convergence;
* traceable recommendations;
* legal verification;
* reproducibility;
* and methodological limitations.

Success does not require unanimous agreement.

Future Extensions

The protocol may later include:

* additional frontier models;
* domain-specific reviewers;
* human expert panels;
* interdisciplinary review boards;
* institutional replication studies;
* and international governance comparisons.

Any additions should preserve reviewer independence and transparent documentation.

Canonical Principle

The modified Delphi protocol transforms AI systems from answer generators into structured reviewers operating within a transparent research methodology.

Its governing sequence is:

Independent Review
↓

↓

↓

↓

The protocol does not seek to prove that AI reviewers are experts.

Instead, it evaluates whether structured multi-model review can responsibly strengthen governance research while preserving uncertainty, transparency, reproducibility, and scholarly integrity.
