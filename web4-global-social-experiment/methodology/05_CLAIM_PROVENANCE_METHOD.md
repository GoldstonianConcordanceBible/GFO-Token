Purpose

This document explains the methodology used to develop, evaluate, document, revise, and preserve claims throughout the Global Social Experiment.

The objective is to ensure that every significant statement appearing in the repository can be traced back through a transparent chain of evidence rather than existing as unsupported interpretation.

Claim provenance is the foundation of reproducible repository-based research.

⸻

Why Claim Provenance Matters

Traditional publications often present conclusions without preserving the complete reasoning process that produced them.

Repository-centered research instead preserves:

* observations;
* supporting evidence;
* analytical reasoning;
* reviewer feedback;
* consensus and dissent;
* legal verification;
* and revision history.

This allows later researchers to understand how a claim evolved rather than only seeing its final wording.

⸻

Definition of a Claim

Within this repository, a claim is any statement that asserts, proposes, interprets, recommends, or concludes something of sufficient importance to influence research, governance, implementation, or publication.

Examples include:

* factual observations;
* methodological conclusions;
* theoretical propositions;
* governance recommendations;
* implementation guidance;
* legal interpretations;
* educational findings;
* and future research recommendations.

Routine editorial language does not require claim tracking.

↓
Evidence Collection
↓
Candidate Claim
↓
Evidence Mapping
↓
Reviewer Evaluation
↓
Consensus and Dissent
↓
Legal Verification (if applicable)
↓
Repository Decision
↓
Canonical Claim

Claims may later be revised or retired.

⸻

Claim Sources

Claims may originate from:

* documentary analysis;
* repository observation;
* interviews where authorized;
* comparative case studies;
* educational activities;
* implementation experience;
* structured LLM review;
* independent replication;
* and researcher synthesis.

Regardless of origin, every significant claim follows the same provenance process.

Evidence Mapping

Every claim should reference one or more evidence items.

Example

claim_id:
CLM-000112
evidence:
EVD-000201
EVD-000233
EVD-000241

Claims should never rely solely on another claim.

Evidence should remain independently identifiable.

⸻

Research Question Mapping

Every claim should identify the research question(s) it addresses.

Example

questions:
RQ36
RQ40
RQ48

This improves traceability, retrieval, and publication mapping.

⸻

Reviewer Evaluation

Reviewer observations should distinguish:

* supported conclusions;
* unsupported assumptions;
* missing evidence;
* alternative interpretations;
* implementation concerns;
* and future research.

Reviewer recommendations become inputs rather than automatic repository changes.

⸻

Consensus and Dissent

Every claim should identify:

consensus:
CON-000041
dissent:
DIS-000012

Where disagreement exists, it remains visible.

Consensus does not erase dissent.

⸻

Legal Verification

Claims involving:

* statutes;
* regulations;
* proposed legislation;
* contractual interpretation;
* or regulatory guidance

should reference the Legal Verification Register.

Legal verification remains independent of reviewer agreement.

⸻

Confidence

Claims may include confidence assessments.

Example

Very High
High
Moderate
Low
Speculative

Confidence reflects current support—not permanent certainty.

Confidence may change as evidence evolves.

⸻

Repository Decisions

Repository editors evaluate whether a candidate claim should become canonical.

Possible outcomes include:

Accepted
Accepted with Revision
Deferred
Requires More Evidence
Rejected
Retired

Every decision should include rationale.

⸻

Claim Revision

Claims may evolve through:

* additional evidence;
* legal updates;
* methodological improvements;
* replication;
* implementation experience;
* or theoretical refinement.

The original claim remains archived.

Revisions do not overwrite research history.

⸻

Claim Retirement

Claims should be retired—not deleted—when:

* evidence no longer supports them;
* legal changes invalidate them;
* methodology is replaced;
* or a more precise claim supersedes them.

Example

status:
Retired
replacement:
CLM-000412
reason:
Superseded by updated evidence

Quality Checklist

Before accepting a claim:

Confirm:

* research question identified;
* evidence mapped;
* claim category assigned;
* reviewer comments preserved;
* consensus recorded;
* dissent recorded if applicable;
* legal verification completed where required;
* repository version assigned;
* publication mapping documented.

Canonical Principle

A repository-centered research system should never ask readers to trust conclusions without showing how those conclusions were developed.

Every important statement should be traceable through:

Question
↓
Evidence
↓
Claim
↓
Review
↓
Consensus and Dissent
↓
Legal Verification
↓
Repository Version
↓
Publication

Claim provenance transforms the repository from a collection of documents into a transparent scholarly knowledge system capable of supporting reproducibility, AI-assisted retrieval, institutional governance, and continuous refinement over time.
